## Synthesis

The founder named Warren and Factory Missions as the closest existing reference points. Their appeal is the ability to define work and step away. Vago aims to let the human use a different Workflow for each Project, including work, solo, and open source Projects, without imposing personal configuration files on collaborators. This describes the founder's desired distinction; the actual capabilities of Warren and Factory Missions have not been checked.

For a shared Project, the founder wants the choice to leave no tracked Vago files or share a `.vago` Workflow intentionally.
The founder might share a Workflow when it has proved useful for a type of work, such as bug fixing.
For the founder, a Workflow is worth sharing when it produces accurate results and runs unattended. Each developer decides how their Workflow checks accuracy; Vago does not supply a universal standard for a correct bug fix.

Candidate formulations, for discussion:

1. Run your Project's own Workflow while you are away.
2. Give each Project its own Workflow, then let Vago carry out the Steps without supervision.
3. Use Vago privately in a shared Project, or share a Workflow when the team wants it.

Open questions:

- Which developers feel the mismatch with prescribed Workflows most strongly?
- What evidence would show that Vago follows a Workflow reliably in real Projects?
- Which differences from Warren and Factory Missions hold up after using those tools directly?

Tensions to revisit:

- The desired position is hands-free execution. Alpha stops on a failed Step and asks the human to inspect and reset it.
- The founder has not checked whether the named alternatives can support the same Workflow and privacy choices.
